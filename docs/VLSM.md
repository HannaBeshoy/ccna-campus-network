# VLSM: subnets sized from the hosts they hold

Generated by `python -m campus vlsm` from network.yaml (ITN module 11). Each VLAN's `hosts` is the
number of devices expected at the real site; every subnet must hold them plus 50% growth plus
3 addresses for the gateways (HSRP virtual address, D1 and D2).

## Step 1: how big must each subnet be

| VLAN | Name | Hosts | + growth + gateways | Smallest prefix | Usable in it |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 10 | STAFF | 40 | 63 | /25 (255.255.255.128) | 126 |
| 20 | PRODUCTION | 90 | 138 | /24 (255.255.255.0) | 254 |
| 30 | IOT | 150 | 228 | /24 (255.255.255.0) | 254 |
| 40 | WIFI | 120 | 183 | /24 (255.255.255.0) | 254 |
| 50 | VOICE | 40 | 63 | /25 (255.255.255.128) | 126 |
| 99 | MGMT | 12 | 21 | /27 (255.255.255.224) | 30 |

Worked example, VLAN 30: 150 hosts + 50% = 225, + 3 gateways = 228. 2^7 - 2 = 126 is too few, 2^8 - 2 = 254 is enough, so 8 host bits and a /24.

## Step 2: allocate the biggest first

Packed from the start of the campus block 10.20.0.0/17, biggest subnet first, so each one
starts on a multiple of its own size and nothing overlaps.

| Subnet for | VLSM subnet | Range | Broadcast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VLAN 20 PRODUCTION | 10.20.0.0/24 | 10.20.0.1 - 10.20.0.254 | 10.20.0.255 |
| VLAN 30 IOT | 10.20.1.0/24 | 10.20.1.1 - 10.20.1.254 | 10.20.1.255 |
| VLAN 40 WIFI | 10.20.2.0/24 | 10.20.2.1 - 10.20.2.254 | 10.20.2.255 |
| VLAN 10 STAFF | 10.20.3.0/25 | 10.20.3.1 - 10.20.3.126 | 10.20.3.127 |
| VLAN 50 VOICE | 10.20.3.128/25 | 10.20.3.129 - 10.20.3.254 | 10.20.3.255 |
| VLAN 99 MGMT | 10.20.4.0/27 | 10.20.4.1 - 10.20.4.30 | 10.20.4.31 |
| R1 G0/1 - D1 | 10.20.4.32/30 | 10.20.4.33 - 10.20.4.34 | 10.20.4.35 |
| R1 G0/2 - D2 | 10.20.4.36/30 | 10.20.4.37 - 10.20.4.38 | 10.20.4.39 |

Everything fits in 1065 addresses, a 10.20.0.0/21 would hold it.

## Step 3: the design keeps /24s, and they are big enough

| VLAN | Design subnet | Room for end devices | Needed with growth | Used |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10 | 10.20.10.0/24 | 234 (DHCP pool) | 60 | 26% |
| 20 | 10.20.20.0/24 | 234 (DHCP pool) | 135 | 58% |
| 30 | 10.20.30.0/24 | 234 (DHCP pool) | 225 | 96% |
| 40 | 10.20.40.0/24 | 234 (DHCP pool) | 180 | 77% |
| 50 | 10.20.50.0/24 | 234 (DHCP pool) | 60 | 26% |
| 99 | 10.20.99.0/24 | 251 (static) | 18 | 7% |

VLAN 30 (IOT) is close to full with the growth margin: past 234 devices it needs a /23 (renumbering) or a second VLAN.

The /24 plan takes 1536 addresses for the VLANs, VLSM would take 1056. Both use a small part of 10.20.0.0/17 (32768 addresses),
so the design spends addresses on readability: the third octet is the VLAN number, every VLAN
has the same .1 gateway and .21-.254 pool, and each DHCP VLAN can grow to 234 devices
without renumbering. VLSM matters when the block is small (a /24 split into VLANs, or public
addresses).

## Point-to-point links: /30 or /31

| Link | /30 (design) | /31 (RFC 3021) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 G0/1 - D1 | 10.20.4.32/30: 10.20.4.33 and 10.20.4.34, 2 of 4 addresses usable | 10.20.4.32/31: 10.20.4.32 and 10.20.4.33, both usable |
| R1 G0/2 - D2 | 10.20.4.36/30: 10.20.4.37 and 10.20.4.38, 2 of 4 addresses usable | 10.20.4.34/31: 10.20.4.34 and 10.20.4.35, both usable |

A /31 halves the addresses a link takes. The design keeps /30s: they are what ITN and SRWE
use, and every device in the lab accepts them.

## IPv6: no VLSM needed

The campus has 2001:db8:acad::/56: 2^(64-56) = 256 subnets of /64. Every VLAN gets a /64
whatever its size (SLAAC needs a 64-bit interface ID), and the subnet ID is the VLAN number
written in hex digits: VLAN 30 is 2001:db8:acad:30::/64.
